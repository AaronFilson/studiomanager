# Interview guide

_For the kickoff and closing interviews in the [pilot kit](README.md). Record each answer against
its ID (S1, Q1, M1, C1), with verbatim quotes where you can._

## Who to interview

The owner or manager first. If the studio has a technician, monitor or co-op officer, talk to one
of them too, even for 15 minutes. The research found that owners volunteer money and burnout
problems, while members and staff hit piece and kiln problems every week
([report, "What hurts"](../planning/reports/Pottery%20studio%20manager%20role.md#what-hurts)). One
voice will miss half of it.

## How to ask

These follow from the gaps in the research, not from a methods textbook.

- **Ask about the last time it happened, not what usually happens.** "When did you last run out of
  clay?" gets a date and a story; "Do you often run out?" gets an opinion. Most of the ten
  questions are already worded this way.
- **Ask for counts, not ratings.** No owner source gave weekly hours (0 of 34), and nothing in the
  research measures kiln-loading or scheduling time. A number with a time window ("about 12
  last week") beats "a lot". If they can't count, ask them to note it in the [diary](diary.md)
  instead of guessing.
- **Ask to see the thing.** The firing log, the clipboard, the spreadsheet, the payout email.
  Public pages under-report back-office work ("not stated" isn't "no"), so the artifact is the
  evidence.
- **Don't pitch at the kickoff.** Don't describe features until Q1–Q10 and M1–M9 are done, so the
  answers aren't shaped by what the app does.
- **Write "don't know" when they don't know.** A blank is better than a guess.
- **Note who answered** (owner, manager, technician, monitor, officer) next to each answer.

## Kickoff interview

About an hour, at least a week before the app is set up.

### Studio profile (5 minutes)

Facts to size the pilot. Fill in what the website already shows before the call and confirm it.

| ID | Question |
|---|---|
| S1 | How many members, students and staff (paid, volunteer, work exchange)? |
| S2 | How many kilns, of what make and controller? How often does each fire? |
| S3 | Who loads the kilns: staff only, certified members, or anyone? |
| S4 | How are firing fees charged (per cubic inch, pound, flat, included), and when are they paid? |
| S5 | Which tools do members book and pay through today? |

### The ten questions from the research report

Verbatim from [the report's "What the evidence can't tell us"](../planning/reports/Pottery%20studio%20manager%20role.md#what-the-evidence-cant-tell-us-and-how-to-find-out),
which wrote them for owners, managers, technicians and co-op officers. The right column is what the
answer feeds; it's for the interviewer, not to read out.

| ID | Question | Feeds |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | What did you do each day last week, and for how long? | Admin minutes baseline; the diary |
| Q2 | When were you last away, what waited, and how did your cover know what to do? | The "studio today" view; the single-point-of-failure finding |
| Q3 | Which tools took money last month, and who reconciles the payouts? | Payout reconciliation; which provider to charge through first |
| Q4 | What does your bookkeeper use, on which plan, and what do they ask you for each month? | Whether live QuickBooks (or Xero) moves up; the CSV export |
| Q5 | How do you know clay or glaze is low, and when did you last run out? | Par levels; stock-outs |
| Q6 | How do you track firings and element changes, and what happened at your last kiln failure? | Firing counts; maintenance |
| Q7 | How many "is it ready?" messages arrive each week? | Ready notices; the member status page |
| Q8 | How are shifts filled, swapped and credited? | Rosters; shift swaps (a Wave 2 candidate) |
| Q9 | What prepaid balances are outstanding? | Stored value (gift cards, firing cards, class packs) |
| Q10 | Whom do you pay besides staff, and how? | Consignment and instructor payables; 1099-NEC export |

Follow-ups worth having ready: for Q3, "how many minutes did the last reconciliation take?"; for
Q7, "by which channel: in person, text, email, social media?"; for Q4, "is it QuickBooks Online,
and which plan?" (on QuickBooks Free, third-party integrations aren't available, and classes and
locations need Plus or Advanced;
[report, H3](../planning/reports/Pottery%20studio%20manager%20role.md#h3-quickbooks-online-integration-stated-requirement)).

### The nine maintenance questions

Verbatim from [05, "Open questions to ask a pilot studio"](../planning/05-maintenance-research.md#open-questions-to-ask-a-pilot-studio).

| ID | Question |
|---|---|
| M1 | Which equipment and building items does someone look after on a schedule? Who does each one: you, a technician, monitors, work exchange, the landlord? |
| M2 | How do you know a kiln needs elements, a thermocouple or relays? Do you count firings, watch firing times, or wait for an error code? When did one last fail mid-firing, and what did it cost? |
| M3 | How often is the clay trap emptied, and by whom? Has a drain ever clogged? |
| M4 | Where is maintenance written down today (paper log, whiteboard, spreadsheet, controller, memory)? Would your cover know what was done last? |
| M5 | Does anyone ask you for maintenance records: an insurer, a landlord, a fire inspector, a school or city, the HSE? |
| M6 | Do you keep spare parts? Did you ever wait on one with a kiln down? |
| M7 | When equipment is down, how do members find out, and does anything stop them booking or loading it? |
| M8 | Would you trust a reminder email to a monitor or work-exchange member, or should tasks always go through a manager? |
| M9 | Is the building yours to maintain (roof, HVAC, extinguishers), or the landlord's? |

M5 mentions the HSE, the UK regulator; for a US studio, ask about OSHA or the fire marshal instead.

### Close the kickoff

- Agree the diary start date and who fills it in. Walk through one example row together.
- Hand over the [studio guide](studio-guide.md).
- Ask: "Is there anything we didn't ask that takes up your week?" Record the answer as K1.

## Closing interview

About 45 minutes, within 3 days of the last diary day. Bring the diary totals, week 0 beside
weeks 1–2, and ask about each difference. Ask about specific events in the diary ("on day 9 you
logged 25 admin minutes; what was that?") rather than general impressions.

| ID | Question | Compare with |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | Here are your admin minutes: week 0 against weeks 1–2. Does that match how the weeks felt? What took the time in your busiest day with the app? | Diary A; Q1 |
| C2 | "Is it ready?" contacts went from X to Y. Which ones still came, and why didn't the email or status page answer them? | Diary B; Q7 |
| C3 | Pick the last batch you sent a ready notice for. How long from unloading the kiln to the notice going out, and what held it up? | Diary C |
| C4 | Did you run low or run out of anything in the three weeks? Did the low-stock alert come before you noticed? | Diary D; Q5 |
| C5 | If you reconciled a payout during the pilot: how long did it take, against the last one before? Would you hand the CSV export to your bookkeeper as it is? | Diary E; Q3, Q4 |
| C6 | Which maintenance tasks came due, which got done, and which reminders did you ignore? Why? | Diary F; M1–M4, M8 |
| C7 | If you were away tomorrow, could your cover run the day from the Today view? What would they still have to ask you? | Q2 |
| C8 | What did you stop doing on paper, and what are you still doing on paper alongside the app? | S2, S4, Q6, M4 |
| C9 | What did members say about it, unprompted? | Q7 |
| C10 | What would make you stop using it? What would you pay for, if anything? | Wave 2 ranking |

End with: "Can we keep in touch, and may we quote you by name or only as 'a US studio'?" Record the
answer; the default is anonymous.
