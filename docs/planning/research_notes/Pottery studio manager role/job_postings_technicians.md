# Job postings: what ceramics technicians are hired to do

_Researched 2026-10-20. Status: complete for this pass. 40 distinct technician postings coded (35 employers; US 35, UK 3, Canada 2, Australia 0, New Zealand 0), plus 3 borderline posts kept out of the denominators. All sources were accessed 2026-10-20._

## TL;DR

- **Equipment upkeep and inventory are the core of the job.** Kiln and equipment maintenance is a listed duty in 38 of 40 postings; kiln firing in 32; inventory, ordering or receiving in 30; glaze and clay mixing or reclaim in 29. Health and safety tasks appear in 21 and supervising students or helpers in 21. Budgets appear in only 6, all in universities, colleges or schools, and none gives an amount.
- **The records are thin and mostly unnamed.** Only 18 of 40 name any explicit document. Inventory records or counts appear in 7 (+1 as a skill), firing logs in 3, maintenance logs in 2 (+2), purchase paperwork in 6, SDS/COSHH files in 3 and risk assessments in 1 (UK). Only 4 name a software product (Microsoft Office, Google Workspace apps, Adobe); none names inventory, maintenance, booking, payment or accounting software. One studio runs on "lots of paper forms".
- **Hypotheses:** inventory tracking is STRONG; asset and equipment tracking is MODERATE (the maintenance work is universal, the record-keeping rarely asked for); scheduling is MODERATE, but as kiln and firing scheduling (13 of 40), not customer booking (3 of 40, one employer); sales integrations with multiple payment providers are WEAK (8 of 40 touch sales, 2 name a POS, 0 name a provider); QuickBooks Online is NONE (0 of 40).
- **Also strong:** technicians run the piece flow (moving and tracking students' and members' work, pickup and discard rules) in 10 of 40, including 6 of 9 community studios and art centres. They are mostly part-time (28 of 40; median 19.5 h/wk) and hourly (US median $20.00/h, n=26).

## Coding scheme

Duty codes (a posting gets a code only if its own text names the duty):

| Code | Duty |
|---|---|
| FIRE | Kiln loading/unloading/firing, firing schedules/programs |
| MAINT | Kiln and equipment maintenance and repair (elements, thermocouples, relays, burners, wheels, pug mills, slab rollers, spray booths, ventilation, dust collection) |
| MIX | Glaze and clay mixing, testing, reclaim |
| INV | Materials inventory, ordering, purchasing, receiving |
| BUD | Managing or monitoring a supplies/equipment budget |
| HS | Health and safety (COSHH, SDS, OSHA, risk assessments, silica control, waste disposal, PPE) |
| SUP | Supervising/training students, members, work-study or other staff |
| SCHED | Scheduling firings, studio access, equipment booking, opening hours |
| DEMO | Technical demonstration / instruction / inductions to students or members |
| CLEAN | Studio cleaning, housekeeping, organisation |
| EXH | Exhibition, degree-show, installation or event support |
| ADMIN | Records, reports, administrative support named as a duty |
| SALES | Selling materials/firing, retail, cash handling |

Record/output codes: firing log, maintenance log, inventory list, purchase orders/requisitions, risk assessments, SDS/COSHH file, induction/training records, budget, reports, other (named verbatim).

Coding conventions: a code with `(q)` means the text appears only as a required skill/knowledge, not as a listed duty. `PIECE` = tracking members' or students' finished work, shelves and pickup. `TEACH` = formal teaching load. Dates are the posting date unless marked "closing". Postings older than 2023 carry their year in bold, e.g. **(2022)**.

## Hypothesis evidence

Ratings are for what ceramics-technician postings show. Technicians are the people who run materials and equipment, so this corpus is the main evidence for H1 and H2 and only side evidence for H3-H5 (owners and front-desk staff, covered elsewhere, may show those needs more strongly).

**H1. Inventory tracking: STRONG (as a recurring job); no incumbent tool named.**
- Inventory, ordering, purchasing or receiving is a listed duty in 30 of 40 postings (75%), +2 more as a required skill; 20 of 27 in 2023-2026; 26 of 35 employers. By segment: 17 of 20 university/college/school, 8 of 9 studios and art centres, but only 5 of 11 municipal posts.
- Ordering/purchasing/receiving is named in 22 of 40; "inventory", "stock checks" or "stock control" as something kept or counted in 24 of 40.
- The work is recurring: "Assist with quarterly clay and dry materials inventory." (T27); "carry out regular stock checks" (T22); "regular and periodic inventory of supplies" (T34). Two postings set par levels and a low-stock trigger: "Mix and de-air clay; maintain 3-4 barrels of prepared clay." and "Notify the Ceramics Studio Manager when materials are low." (T39; the 2015 version T13 has the same rules).
- It covers both consumables (clay, glaze chemicals, dry materials) and equipment ("Provide updated inventory list of equipment", T01; "departmental inventories of instructional supplies and equipment", T08).
- What is missing: only 7 of 40 name an explicit inventory record, list or count; 0 of 40 name inventory software; 3 name a spreadsheet; 1 uses paper forms ("The studio uses lots of paper forms to track things like clay inventory, firing, orientations etc.", T03). 0 of 40 give a clay tonnage or materials spend. So the postings prove the job exists and recurs, but not how many hours it takes.
- Evidence against: 8 of 40 never mention inventory, 6 of them municipal or teaching-studio posts that are firing-and-glaze jobs.

**H2. Asset and equipment tracking: MODERATE (the maintenance work is universal; the record-keeping is rarely asked for).**
- Kiln and equipment maintenance or repair is a duty in 38 of 40 (95%); 26 of 27 recent; 33 of 35 employers. It is the most common duty in the corpus.
- But a maintenance log, schedule or repair record is named in only 2 of 40 ("Maintain accurate firing logs, material records, and equipment maintenance schedules.", T09; Solano's records of "equipment operation, maintenance and repair", T21), +2 as skills ("accurately document maintenance and repair activities using manual and computerized systems.", T32; maintenance and ordering schedules, T29). An equipment inventory list appears once (T01) and an equipment check-out facility once (T32). A university asset register appears only in a borderline sculpture post (B03, "Work with Office of Asset Management to properly log equipment and oversee inventory audits.").
- Repairs often go outside: 7 of 40 postings (5 employers) involve arranging vendors or contractors (T08, T10, T14-T16 quotes from "maintenance contractors", T28, T37), and 1 rules repair out ("The Studio Tech is not responsible for repairing kilns and other equipment.", T03).
- The number of kilns is stated in only 1 of 40 (T17: four large electric kilns and one small glass kiln); T31 and T32 list kiln types without a number.
- Read: an equipment register with a per-kiln maintenance and repair log (elements, relays/switches, controllers, vendor visits) matches real work, but no posting asks for an asset-management system. Frame it as maintenance history, not fixed-asset accounting.

**H3. Sales integrations with multiple payment providers: WEAK for this role; no evidence for the "multiple providers" part.**
- Any sales, cash or payment task: 8 of 40 (20%); 6 of 35 employers. Point of sale named in 2 (T03 "Ring up customer sales (clay sales, firing fees, day-passes, etc.) on the register (POS).", T34 "Locate, order, purchase, and resale all general-use supplies using a point of sale system."); cashier 1 (T11); payments and member accounts 1 (T25 "Processing payments, creating/updating member/customer accounts"); patron registration and clay sales 3 (T14-T16, one employer); reclaimed clay sold to students 1 (T20).
- Payment providers named (Stripe, Square or any other): 0 of 40. Online sales: 0 of 40.
- University posts handle money as lab fees and budgets (T04 "Manage lab fee budget"), not customer checkout.
- Read: where technicians take money it is over-the-counter sale of clay, firing fees and day passes, on whatever register the studio already has. That argues for "record a sale against the studio's existing POS" over building several payment integrations in phase 1.

**H4. QuickBooks Online integration: NONE in this corpus; mild evidence against it as a technician need.**
- Accounting software named: 0 of 40.
- Budget work appears in 6 of 40, all in universities, colleges or schools (T02, T04, T08, T21, T22, T33); municipal 0 of 11; studios and art centres 0 of 9. No posting states a budget amount.
- The money paperwork technicians produce is institutional purchasing: purchase requisitions, POs, quotes and invoices in 6 of 40 (T04, T14-T16, T21, T34), with approval held by others ("Work with department faculty and department head to order materials and supplies.", T37; "Requisitioning quotes and reviewing invoices from pottery suppliers and maintenance contractors.", T14). Only 4 of 40 hold a budget themselves; 2 more spend within one.
- Read: a technician-facing phase 1 would need a purchase-request and receiving record, not a QBO sync. Whether owners need QBO is a question for the owner/manager research, not this corpus.

**H5. Scheduling: MODERATE, but for kiln and firing scheduling, not customer booking.**
- Scheduling duty: 15 of 40 (38%); 11 of 27 recent; 12 of 35 employers. Of these, firing schedules 13 ("Developing firing schedule and adhering to it on a weekly basis.", T14-T16; "Maintain kiln firing schedule for large amounts of clay projects from multiple classes", T17; "Creating a firing schedule based on the work being produced;", T12; "daily firing schedules", T29); equipment-use scheduling 2 (T10, T11); studio usage or access 2 (T16, T08).
- Strongly segment-dependent: 9 of 11 municipal posts, 3 of 9 studios and art centres, 3 of 20 universities.
- Customer-facing booking: class or patron registration appears in 3 of 40, all one employer (Gwinnett); 0 of 40 name booking software. Open-studio supervision (staffing hours) is common inside SUP (e.g. T13, T19, T25, T37, T39).
- Firing scheduling is tied to moving members' and students' work, which appears in 10 of 40 (see Other strong signals). Read: a firing queue/kiln calendar is supported; class and membership booking is not something technician postings ask for.

## Other strong signals

1. **Moving and tracking people's work is a technician job.** 10 of 40 postings (6 of 9 community studios and art centres) assign it: "Keep track of fired goods throughout the studio, rotating them onto the Rack of Doom" "and discarding uncollected wares after 60 days." (T03); "Coordinate pick up area for finished artwork." (T17); "Maintain work-flow systems to track and organize student work." (T28); "Help move student work through the firing cycles in a timely fashion." (T27); Clackamas puts 30% of the job on "Processing Student Work" (T20). This matches the top pain in `01-market-survey.md` (piece tracking) and names the person who would use a piece tracker.
2. **Firing is near-universal, but firing records are not.** FIRE in 32 of 40, but a firing log or firing record in only 3 (T09 firing logs; T03 firing forms and Firing Submission Forms; T20 "Track history of temperature increase for firing processes"). Five postings mention computerized or programmed kilns and one names kiln brands; none names a controller app. Consistent with `03-tools-in-use.md` (no controller APIs, paper firing slips).
3. **Materials prep is constant and could drive inventory automatically.** Glaze and clay mixing, testing or reclaim in 29 of 40; reclaimed clay is re-bagged and sold in T20 ("for sale to students through means of Pug Mill"); par levels in T13/T39. Inference, not stated in any posting: logging glaze batches and reclaim against dry-materials stock would keep inventory current without separate counts.
4. **Technicians are part-time, hourly and low-paid.** 28 of 40 are part-time; median 19.5 h/wk (n=19, range 6-25), with three caps just under 20 hours; 10 of 40 temporary, seasonal, contract or at-will; 4 explicitly without benefits. US hourly median $20.00 (n=26; $21.00 for 2023-2026, n=17). Many work evenings and weekends. Any tool for them has to be quick, phone-friendly and need little training.
5. **They supervise helpers.** SUP in 21 of 40; work-study or student employees named in 7 (+1 skill), up to "8-10 Work Study students" (T31); apprentices and studio monitors (T27); an assistant program (T28). Task lists, checklists and shift rosters for helpers are a real adjacent need.
6. **Health and safety paperwork is a university and UK theme.** HS duties in 21 of 40 (14 of 20 in universities/colleges/schools). SDS/MSDS/COSHH files named in 3 (T11, T21, T22), risk assessments in 1 (T22, UK), induction/orientation records in 1 (T03). A small SDS register and orientation log is cheap to build but the evidence is thin outside education.
7. **Purchasing authority is small and approval sits elsewhere.** 4 of 40 hold a budget; none states an amount; requisitions, quotes and "with ... department head" wording show approvals elsewhere. A purchase-request, approve, receive flow fits better than accounting sync.
8. **Recurring cadences are explicit.** Weekly firing schedules (3), weekly deep cleans (2), weekly sink buckets (1), monthly floor deep cleans (2), quarterly inventory (1), quarterly deep clean (1), annual work plan (1). Recurring maintenance and cleaning checklists with intervals map directly onto these.
9. **The three segments want different things.** Universities/colleges/schools: budgets (6 of 20, the only segment with any), inventory and purchasing (17 of 20), H&S (14 of 20), supervising and training students (13 of 20). Municipal centres: firing schedules (9 of 11), registration and cashier (4 of 11), little inventory (5 of 11). Community studios and art centres: piece flow (6 of 9), inventory and ordering (8 of 9), POS and member accounts (2 of 9). A phase 1 has to pick a primary segment or support roles per segment.
10. **Records are mostly paper or unspecified.** Only 18 of 40 name any explicit document; only 4 name a software product (Microsoft Office incl. Excel, Google Workspace incl. Sheets, Adobe). One posting describes "lots of paper forms" that "should be checked daily" (T03).

## Gaps and limits

- **Search budget.** Only 9 web searches ran before the session-wide search cap was hit. All later discovery used job boards' own search pages (NEOGOV, Chronicle, Inside Higher Ed, TES, jobs.ac.uk, Jora, Job Bank) and aggregator archives (Ceramics Now, UW art advising blog, Akimbo, arts.ca.gov). The corpus reflects what those boards index.
- **True count.** 40 coded postings (35 employers) plus 3 borderline posts kept out of the denominators (B01 Columbus GA Pottery Specialist, B02 Kirkland Arts Center Ceramics Studio Manager, B03 UAlbany sculpture technician).
- **Geography.** US 35, UK 3, Canada 2 (one employer), Australia 0, New Zealand 0. Seek and ArtsHub returned 403; Jora AU showed no studio or university ceramics technician posts on 2026-10-20; Jora NZ returned 410. UK: jobs.ac.uk removes expired ads and had no ceramics technician ad live on 2026-10-20; the Crafts Council board returned 403.
- **Segments.** PYOP, production pottery, makerspace and cooperative: 0 postings. The producer and PYOP careers pages checked (Heath Ceramics, East Fork, Color Me Mine) listed no technician openings; East Fork's ADP portal was not attempted, and Indeed, where much small-employer hiring is posted, was not tried. No makerspace or cooperative source was reachable without web search.
- **Archives and PDFs.** WebFetch refuses web.archive.org, so no Wayback snapshot could be used. PDF job descriptions came back as undecodable binary (The Umbrella, Place des Arts 2026, Edinburgh council 2014), so other PDFs (NSCAD 2019, West Point Grey 2026) were not attempted.
- **Age.** 13 of 40 postings predate 2023 (2012-2022) and are labelled; 27 are 2023-2026 or current. Recent-only counts are given beside the all-years counts.
- **Reposts.** 19 of 40 were read on aggregator reposts (Ceramics Now 8, UW advising blog 7, Akimbo 2, arts.ca.gov 1, Webrecruit 1), not the employer's own page; employer pages had expired or were JavaScript-only.
- **Quote fidelity.** WebFetch returns a model-processed view and caps quotes at about 125 characters, so long bullets appear as fragments. Quotes are exactly as the tool returned them; raw HTML was not inspected.
- **Coding judgement.** Duty vs required skill ("(q)") depends on posting format (duty list vs knowledge/ability list, e.g. T07, T32). HS was coded only for a specific safety task, not generic "safe and clean" wording; SCHED excludes cleaning schedules and event calendars; INV includes "stocking materials" (T40) and "material ... management" (T07).
- **Re-advertisements.** Gwinnett County's role appears 3 times (2022, 2023, 2024; more than 6 months apart, so counted per the brief), which adds 2 to FIRE, MAINT, MIX, INV, SCHED and SALES counts. One-row-per-employer counts are in the sensitivity table.
- **What postings cannot show.** They show what employers ask for, not hours spent. Frequency is stated in only 15 of 40. No posting gives a budget amount or clay tonnage.
