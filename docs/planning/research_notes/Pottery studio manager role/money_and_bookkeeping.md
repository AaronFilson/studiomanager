# Money and Bookkeeping: How Money Moves Through a Pottery Studio

_Research notes, started 2026-10-20. Working file: findings are appended as sources are read. Builds on [03-tools-in-use.md](../../03-tools-in-use.md) (84-studio census) and [01-market-survey.md](../../01-market-survey.md); does not redo them._

## Headline findings (details and sources below)

- **Studios sell many kinds of thing.** All 23 studio sites read sell classes. 20 sell gift cards, 18 memberships or passes, 13 events, 12 rentals and 9 outside firing. The median studio shows 6 of 11 revenue lines (B3).
  - Among 12 US clay nonprofits, earned program revenue is the majority at 8. Contributions are ≥20% of revenue at 9 (B1).
- **Multiple payment tools are common but not universal.** 21 of 84 census studios (25%) take money through two or more tools (C1).
  - Square is never the only one, and is usually there for gift cards or a clay store.
  - Clover and Etsy: 0 sightings (C2).
- **The bookkeeping pain is the join between payouts and sales.** 55 independent first-person posters (2022–2026) describe it.
  - Top problems: matching payouts to sales (15), keying entries by hand (9), fees (8), missing bank feeds (6), summary vs. itemized (5), tax-category reporting (5) and duplicated revenue (4).
  - Inventory levels not updating: 0 (E-summary).
- **QuickBooks is the only ledger pottery sources name**: 3 practitioners plus 1 unverified job posting (D1). Intuit is ~92% US by revenue (D4.1).
  - Outside the US, Xero is at least as likely: 2.7m ANZ subscriptions (D4.6).
  - One accountant, several sellers, and Square's own new integration favour **daily summary journal entries** (E1e.3, E1.14, E1g.1, E1.16).
- **Tax treatment varies by item, place and date.** Washington made group pottery classes taxable from 2025-10-01 and exempted 501(c) presenters again from 2026-07-01. California taxes firing; Virginia (1987) did not when firing was sold separately (A1).
- **Stored value and payables are under-served money objects.**
  - Stored value: gift cards, firing cards, class packs, memberships, deposits.
  - Payables: instructor shares, consignment commissions, 1099s.
  - Neither appears in the phase-1 hypotheses (B2, B2c, E1d).

Tax treatment therefore has to be set per item category and per studio, and must be changeable by date, not hard-coded as "services untaxed, goods taxed".

**Implications (A6).** A studio's outstanding gift cards stay on the books as a liability for at least five years (longer where state law bans expiry; California's Civil Code 1749.5 could not be opened, 403). Tax rules allow at most a one-year deferral of prepaid class packs and memberships, while book (GAAP) deferral follows delivery, so the bookkeeper may need both a "deferred revenue" schedule and a tax adjustment. Instructor status is decided case by case in every country, and California's ABC test makes the common "1099 instructor" pattern (B2b) legally risky there.

**Cross-country pattern (A1–A4).** In all four countries the default for a for-profit studio is: recreational classes taxable *if* the studio is registered (UK, CA, AU) or the state taxes them (US: WA since 2025-10-01; most states still don't). Charities and NPOs get carve-outs (UK eligible body; CA kids' programmes; WA 501(c) from 2026-07-01; AU $150k threshold). Registration thresholds (£90k, C$30k, A$75k, NZ$60k) mean many small studios charge no VAT/GST at all, but a growing one crosses the line mid-year. Gift vouchers are taxed at redemption in CA and AU, and at redemption or issue in the UK depending on what they can buy.

**What this shows.** A firing card is stored value: a liability that is drawn down per firing, refilled, partly refunded to the card (with a fee) when pieces are re-measured, and valid five years (matching Regulation E, A6.7). The bookkeeper needs: balance outstanding (liability), draws per period (revenue), refills, card-credited adjustments and fees, and the tax collected at sale. This is the same structure as gift cards and class packs, so one stored-value ledger could serve all three.

### H1. Inventory tracking (clay, glaze, retail stock) — **weak**

- **For.** Clay, glaze or tool sales appear on 6 of 23 studio sites (+3 partial) (B3). One of 12 clay nonprofits has material inventory sales: Archie Bray, $536,582, 25.4% of revenue (B1). Consignment needs an intake list per artist (The Clay Studio's "Inventory Intake spreadsheet", B2.1). 03 shows clay sold with firing bundled in (Mud Studio, Recess; Pickering's tickets per bag of clay), so a clay sale can carry a firing obligation. One advice article (PMI, Nov/Dec 2024) recommends inventory software (D1.3); that is advice, not use.
- **Against.** 8 of 12 nonprofit 990s show $0 inventory sales and 3 more show ≤1.6% (B1). Finished-work retail is explicit on only 3 of 23 sites (B3). In the bookkeeping-pain set, **0 of 55** posters complain that inventory levels don't update; **2 of 55** raise inventory at all, both retailers, and both about purchase orders and COGS (E1f). The bookkeeping practice found keeps item-level stock in the operating system and posts only a summary inventory/COGS entry to the ledger (E1f.2). 01 ranked material inventory "low"; 03 found no studio using Sortly or Craftybase.
- **Reading.** For the money side, stock tracking matters to a minority of studios (those with a clay store or a shop). Two narrower needs are better evidenced: a **consignment intake and commission ledger** (B2) and **clay sold with firing included** (03).

### H2. Asset and equipment tracking — **weak** (none for asset registers and depreciation)

- **For.** Equipment earns money directly. Studio, kiln or wheel rental is explicit at 12 of 23 sites (+3) (B3: "Kiln Hire", "Wheel hire", "Equipment Hire"). Damage charges are levied on equipment: Feats of Clay's "$40" shelf penalty (B2c-bis); Atelier Forma's kiln damage fee (03). MudFire grew by "building new storage and shelving" (B2d.1). Capital funds exist at nonprofits: Mudflat's "Building & Equipment Fund" (B2a) and Pottery Northwest's contribution swing (B1).
- **Against.** 0 of 55 bookkeeping-pain posters, 0 of 4 pottery voices in E3, and 0 of 3 ceramics job postings opened mention a fixed-asset register, depreciation or kiln asset records.
- **Reading.** The money-relevant asset need is **bookable, chargeable resources** (rental rates, damage fees, capacity) and **capital-fund tagging**, not an asset register.

### H3. Sales integrations with multiple payment providers — **moderate** (for importing payouts); **weak** (for taking payments through several providers)

- **For.** **21 of 84 census studios (25%)** show two or more payment-taking tools (C1). All 7 Square studios also run another system. 5 use a marketplace (ClassBento, Eventbrite) that collects money and pays out later. A single storefront can add PayPal next to its own processor (C1, Maker's Loft). Matching payouts and deposits to sales is the **most common bookkeeping complaint: 15 of 55 posters** (E-summary). Multi-channel sellers say the problem multiplies (KP_Enterprises; peterrobert1261: "month-end was a mess"). A booking tool feeding a processor loses sales categories (Acuity → Square, E1f.4).
- **Against / nuance.** 63 of 84 census studios (75%) show one payment-taking tool or none. Kiln Fire, the most-adopted pottery tool, is "100% tied into Stripe" (D2). Clover appears **0** times and Etsy **0** times; PayPal appears **1** time, as a checkout option (C2). The second provider is usually **Square for gift cards or a clay store** (5 of 7 Square studios) or a **marketplace payout**, not a second card processor for the same sales.
- **Reading.** Evidence supports reading payouts, fees and refunds from **two or three sources** (the studio's own processor, Square gift cards or POS, a marketplace) into one reconciled ledger. Evidence does not support building payment-taking integrations for many processors in phase 1.

### H4. QuickBooks Online integration — **moderate in the US; contradicts as the only ledger outside the US**

- **For.** QuickBooks is the **only ledger named in pottery sources**: 3 practitioner sources (Pottery West [UK], Burst of Butterflies [US], Past Lives Makerspace [US]) + 1 unverified job posting (Claymakers [US]), plus 1 advice article (D1). Intuit earns ~92% of revenue in the US (D4.1). Of the 17 Square-community posters in E1, **12** are QuickBooks users with sync problems, and 4 more Square sellers in the QuickBooks Community describe the same thing (E1g). A studio tool that posts correct entries to QuickBooks would remove real work. When Square's QuickBooks connector dropped its summary option in August 2025, a seller wrote "I can not review hundreds of individual transactions each day" (E1g.1). A makerspace with ceramics asks to "hand the month to QuickBooks" (E1d). Square has rebuilt its own QuickBooks integration around **daily summary journal entries** (E1.16), and an accountant asked for the "journal entry method" (E1e.3).
- **Against / nuance.**
  - **Studios themselves rarely raise accounting.** 0 of the 4 pottery posters in E3 mention bookkeeping; 03 found one accounting mention in total (Pottery West) and none among its owner quotes; 0 of 3 ceramics job postings opened name accounting software.
  - **Outside the US, Xero is at least as likely.** Xero has 2.7m ANZ subscriptions against ~2.8m Australian businesses, and an unverified ~1.2m in the UK (D4.6–D4.7). 4 of the 17 E1 posters use Xero.
  - **Cost and lock-in.** 2 posters object to QuickBooks' price or lock-in; others use Zoho Books, Odoo or FreshBooks (E1f.5).
  - **The biggest gap is payables, not sales sync.** Past Lives' unmet need is a bill or journal-entry import for instructor and payee payouts, with W-9/1099 status (E1d).
  - **Per-transaction sync is itself the problem.** It produces the duplicates, fee and mapping problems in E1/E1b.
- **Reading.** A US-first QuickBooks Online connector is justified, if it posts **daily or per-payout summary journal entries** (revenue by stream, tax by category, fees, gift-card and deferred-revenue movements, clearing-account deposits) and **bills for payees**. Build it on a ledger-neutral export (CSV/JE), so Xero, the likely second ledger for UK, AU and NZ studios, and UK "bridging" filers (26% of below-threshold VAT businesses, D4.3) are not locked out.

### H5. Scheduling — **moderate** (as the source of money events); **weak** (as a new booking product)

- **For.** Classes appear on **23 of 23** studio sites; parties and events on 13 (+4); rentals on 12 (+3) (B3). Bookings create money events: deposits and tiered cancellation fees (Lygon Street; MudFire's refundable deposits; Bonbon's holds and no-show fees, B2c-bis/E3.5); instructor revenue shares paid "48 hours after a paid class's first session starts" (E1d); deferred revenue recognised per attended session (F6). Washington taxes **group** classes but not one-on-one instruction (A1.4), so the booking record (participant count) decides the tax. Square Appointments doesn't handle group classes (E3.2).
- **Against.** 03 found studios already book through storefronts and schedulers (33 + 11 of 84). 0 of 55 bookkeeping posts ask for scheduling.
- **Reading.** Scheduling matters to bookkeeping because sessions are when revenue is earned, instructors are owed, and (in WA) tax applies. That argues for importing or recording session delivery and attendance, not for replacing the studio's booking tool (consistent with 03).

## Other strong signals

1. **Prepaid stored value is the core money object.**
   - Where it shows up: gift cards on 20 of 23 sites; Choplet's refillable firing card (5-year validity, tax collected at sale by inference, a 16% "restocking fee"); class packs; minimum-commitment memberships ($215–270/month); deposits and 4-payment plans; Mudflat's $200 non-renewing subscription.
   - Rules that govern it: Regulation E's 5-year minimum; HMRC single- vs multi-purpose vouchers; CRA and ATO tax at redemption (ATO unverified); IRS one-year tax deferral cap.
   - Implication: one **stored-value / deferred-revenue ledger with a roll-forward report** (opening, sold, used, expired, adjusted, closing) serves gift cards, firing cards, packs and memberships. (B2c, B2c-bis, A2.3, A3.4, A6.6–A6.7, F6)
2. **Payables matter as much as receivables.**
   - Instructor revenue shares (Past Lives 70/10/20 and 70/15/15).
   - Consignment commissions (The Clay Studio 50/50 via bill.com monthly; 30–60% elsewhere, unverified).
   - Contract instructors (BKLYN CLAY "1099 Independent Contractor"; 1099-NEC threshold $2,000 from 2026).
   - The contractor-to-W-2 shift (MudFire).
   - Staff paid partly in studio credit ($100/month firing fees at BKLYN CLAY; work-exchange programmes).
   - None of the published hypotheses covers this. (B2, B2b, B2d, E1d, A5)
3. **Tax must be per item category, per jurisdiction and date-effective.**
   - Washington taxed pottery classes from 2025-10-01 and exempted 501(c) presenters from 2026-07-01.
   - Firing is taxable "fabrication" in California; it was a non-taxable separate service in Virginia (1987).
   - UK classes are exempt only from eligible bodies; Canadian recreational courses are taxable, while NPO children's programmes are exempt.
   - Registration thresholds: £90k, C$30k/$50k, A$75k/$150k, NZ$60k.
   - Practitioners already struggle: Clarion West charged every Washington buyer the Seattle rate, and 5 of 55 posters struggled with taxable, non-taxable and mixed-rate reporting. (A1–A4, E-summary)
4. **Nonprofit studios are fund-accounting businesses.**
   - 9 of 12 clay nonprofits take ≥20% of revenue from contributions; 4 of 12 are majority-contributed.
   - Mudflat lets donors designate 6 funds.
   - Capital campaigns swing revenue year to year.
   - A fund tag on donations, grants and restricted spending is needed for this segment. (B1, B2a)
5. **Usage-based charges accumulate into a monthly bill.** TCLA wants to "charge once a month to a stored credit card"; Morning, Berkeley, Kilnfolk and Hayden invoice monthly (03). The balance-owed ledger in 02 is the right core, and it should export as receivables or as revenue lines. (E3.1)
6. **Integrations fail in the gaps between vendors.**
   - In every thread with a vendor reply, the vendor sends the seller elsewhere: Square staff point to QuickBooks, Xero or Amaka, and QuickBooks support sent one seller back to Square.
   - The best-documented workarounds: clearing accounts, entries keyed by hand (9 posters), and CSV exports.
   - Openings for a tool that **owns the mapping and shows a reconciliation report** (deposit = sales − refunds − fees ± adjustments):
     - Kiln Fire advertises no accounting integration (D2).
     - The beancount.io blog is the only pottery-specific bookkeeping guidance found (D3).
   - (E-summary, F4)
7. **UK compulsion.** Making Tax Digital for Income Tax now covers sole traders over £50k (from April 2026), extending to £30k in 2027 and £20k in 2028: 864k, 1,077k and 975k individuals. Clean CSV and journal exports that bridging software and Xero, QuickBooks or FreeAgent can take will matter to UK studio owners. (D4.5)
8. **No evidence for Clover or Etsy at studio level.** Both appear 0 times in 84 + 23 studio sources (C2). Finished work is sold through the studio's own shop or gallery, or by members themselves.
