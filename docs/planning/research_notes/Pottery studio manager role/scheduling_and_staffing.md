- **Trade value was recalibrated between 2021 and 2026.** VisArts blog, 2021-08-19 **(pre-2024)**, https://www.visarts.org/?p=14903221: "Studio monitors volunteer in the studio of their choice for at least five hours per week"; "Studio monitors must commit to volunteering for at least one complete, 12-week session."; they earned "One free class per session (for use during the session in which they volunteer or the session immediately following it)" plus "Free open studio access during the session in which they volunteer". **G3. Legal frame (affects how work exchange can be recorded and valued)**
- **US Department of Labor (primary)**, https://webapps.dol.gov/elaws/whd/flsa/docs/volunteers.asp (undated, accessed 2026-10-20): "Under the FLSA, employees may not volunteer services to for-profit private sector employers." Non-profit volunteers are those who serve for "public service" / "religious or humanitarian objectives" "not as employees and without contemplation of pay".
- Ward and Smith (law firm, 2014-12-18 **(pre-2024)**): nonprofit volunteers may receive "reimbursement for expenses, reasonable benefits, and nominal fees" without becoming employees.
- UNVERIFIED (403): Ask a Manager, July 2025, on a for-profit yoga studio's volunteers: "it's actually illegal for them to use volunteers for anything; for-profit businesses are required to pay people at least minimum wage for all work that they perform", with notes that barter value may be taxable and that California is stricter.
- Implication for pottery: most work-exchange programs found sit in **nonprofits or co-ops** (VisArts, Pottery NW, CAW, WSW, Merrill, Stanford, Copper Country, Sonoma, Oberlin) while **for-profit studios pay hourly technicians who receive studio access as a perk** (NW Pottery Collective $20–25/h, Throw & Build $23.50/h, Creative Hands $19–25/h). In the UK, two for-profit-looking studios run exchanges (The Kiln Rooms Ltd, 18–20 people × 7 h/week; Clay Shed, 4 h/week); UK rules were not researched. Software that "tracks work-exchange hours" for a for-profit US studio may be tracking unpaid wages; a hours-to-credit ledger needs to support both "volunteer" (nonprofit) and "paid staff with perks" modes.

## Hypothesis evidence

Scope note: this file covers scheduling and staffing. Evidence on the four non-scheduling hypotheses is what surfaced incidentally in ~53 pottery organisations' scheduling, policy and job pages, plus 26 labour-program pages. 03 and 01 hold the main commerce and tooling evidence.

**1. Inventory tracking: WEAK.** It is a recurring duty (8 of 15 role descriptions) but not a voiced pain (0 complaints).
- For: 8 of 15 job/role descriptions read include stock, ordering or inventory (Kirkland "Keep all supplies stocked (clay, glazes, tools, class supplies)"; CAC Wallingford "Purchasing supplies for the ceramic program within budget limits."; Castle Hill "Cleaning, inventory and closing the studios at the end of the season."; Merrill managers "ordering supplies… facilities organization and inventory"; Creative Hands, Throw & Build and NW Pottery Collective (paraphrased duties); NCC volunteers "evaluate gallery inventory"). 6 sources show clay/material sales on the floor tied to shifts or tickets (Arts Underground monitors "sells clay"; Sonoma monitors "sell materials"; Copper Country front desk; Springfield "Sign out the clay in the log, including payment type."; Pickering tickets issued per bag of clay with a week-7 clay sales cutoff; Fitton restock post). PMI 2024 advises inventory systems.
- Against: 0 first-person complaints about inventory in this scope; 01 ranked materials inventory 9th of 10.
- Implication: inventory matters to scheduling where **clay sales generate firing entitlements** (Pickering) or happen **during monitor shifts** (POS on the floor).

**2. Asset and equipment tracking: WEAK** as a standalone asset register. The same evidence gives moderate support to modelling equipment as bookable resources with permissions, rentals and maintenance blocks inside scheduling.
- For: equipment maintenance in 5 of 15 role descriptions (NW Pottery Collective, Kirkland "equipment assessments, routine maintenance", CAC Wallingford, Castle Hill, The Clay Studio "kiln maintenance"); equipment rented out in 5 sources (kiln rentals at WSW, Belger, The Clay Studio, Saint John; take-home wheel hire at The Kiln Rooms, "£75 per week", "minimum of 4 weeks", "£250 refundable deposit"); equipment permission gates in 8 sources (UCSD kiln test, NCC self-firing, Mudflat residents, Belger renter training, North Boroughs "authorized staff", Kiln Rooms exchange kiln training, Clay Shed self-firing only on the Unlimited tier, Lygon Street induction for "Slab rollers, Pug mill, Extruder"). PMI 2024: "If a kiln breaks down unexpectedly, it can disrupt the entire studio's schedule".
- Against: 0 first-person complaints about losing track of equipment; no studio describes an asset register, tags or maintenance log software.

**3. Sales integrations with multiple payment providers: WEAK.** No source asks for multi-provider integration; there is moderate support for a narrower need, taking payment at the moment of booking.
- For payment-at-booking: 4 of 6 party/rental sources require deposits or prepayment; prepaid passes or punch cards at 3 studios (Brookline pass packs, Turning Earth pass packs, Copper Country punch cards) and pay-per-booking at Canberra ($11.30 per session, non-refundable); no-show charges at Brookline; vendor claim (Acuity/Mud Hut) that paying at booking "helps reduce no-shows".
- For multiple channels: 17 payment channels appear across 15 studios in this scope, counting each studio's in-person desk separately (ActiveNet at Brookline; Canberra's member area; Ticket Tailor at Dublin; GetGalore at Recess; PlaceFull at ClayTime; Create Art's booking portal; Turning Earth's booking system; a shop cart and Shop Pay Installments at NCC; BACS at Kiln Rooms; check/money order only at Merrill; a cash box and front-office card/check at Springfield; the front desk at Copper Country; payment at hand-in at Quarry; monitor-collected drop-in payments at Arts Underground; a "GHP measuring app"). One studio commonly takes money through 2–3 of these (Springfield, NCC, Copper Country).
- Against: 0 sources in this scope name Stripe or Square, and 0 complain about connecting payment providers. Most channels are the booking platform's own payments, so a scheduler that does not take payments leaves the money in another tool.

**4. QuickBooks Online integration: NONE in this scope.**
- 0 of ~53 pottery organisations and 0 of 26 labour-program pages mention QuickBooks or any accounting tool. Mentions come only from a vendor (Classly: "QuickBooks Online, Squarespace, WordPress, Wix, Shopify, and more", 2023) and trade-press advice (PMI 2024). This is absence in scheduling pages, not evidence against accounting needs generally (03 found one potter naming QuickBooks).

**5. Scheduling: STRONG** for kiln queues, labour rosters, orientation gating and a coupled studio calendar. The evidence **CONTRADICTS** a build that leads with class or wheel booking.
- For: 2 of 3 studio-manager postings bundle kiln scheduling, open-studio scheduling and volunteer/work-study/technician rosters in one role (the third assists with firings); 14 studios publish kiln queue rules (11 verified) and 13 state turnaround or firing-timing terms (10 with a figure); kiln work recurs at least weekly; 6 first-person kiln complaints vs 0 for wheel booking; 0 of 16 pottery labour programs use a scheduling tool; 14 sources show class/open-studio/kiln/event/staffing conflicts; 12 require scheduled orientations.
- Contradicting a booking-first build: class, party and pass booking is already served by commodity tools (03: 55 of 84 studios), 4 of 11 studios with open-studio rules run without reservations, and vendors (not studios) supply most of the wheel-booking pain claims (4 VC vs 0 FP).
- So the scheduling scope most supported by evidence is **kiln queue/calendar + labour rosters with hour ledgers + orientation/certification gating + one coupled resource calendar with change notifications**. Class and wheel booking should integrate with or import from existing tools rather than lead.

## Other strong signals

1. **Kiln capacity is rationed by written policy** at 14 studios (11 verified): class work first (5), in-house before outsiders (3), wait for a full kiln/minimum fill (2 verified), spread across members (1 verified via 03), volume caps (Canberra 10 kg/week; Pickering 6 pieces/week; Jotform two boxes), blackout months (Springfield June and December), week-number cutoffs (Pickering week 8; Wesleyan October). A configurable queue-policy engine is a differentiator no vendor page claims.
2. **Calendars are coupled.** Classes or events pre-empt open studio (Pottery NW, ASP, Mudflat, NCC, Recess, Springfield, Dublin, North Boroughs, Square Edge: 9 sources; Canberra also closes member sessions in term breaks and moves them around events); a running kiln closes the studio (Square Edge); staffing decides availability (Mississauga wheel only "if there is an Open Studio Leader available"; Sonoma evenings unmonitored; Fitton cancellations); events consume kiln capacity (Kiln Rooms glazes and fires 3–4 pieces per party guest).
3. **The term is the unit of scheduling.** Rosters per 10–12-week session (VisArts), quarters (NW Pottery Collective, Merrill, Stanford, Pottery NW), school terms (Canberra, Clay Talk), semesters (Hammond, Wesleyan, CAW), years (Waterloo, Mississauga). Rules change between terms (VisArts 2021→2026), so rules need versioning by term.
4. **Work exchange is a nonprofit/co-op pattern in the US and a large for-profit pattern in the UK.** USDOL: "employees may not volunteer services to for-profit private sector employers." Implied trade values (Clay Shed ≈ £7.50–£9.00 and Pottery NW ≈ $14.40 per work hour) sit below paid technician wages ($19–25/h). Hours convert into access, class seats (WSW, JMBT 1:1, SADC 1:1) or credits (Lacey: membership credit per 9 h).
5. **Studios improvise tools** at least 10 ways: a shop checkout for volunteer shifts (NCC), a Ticket Tailor member code (Dublin), Jotform and monday.com intake forms, a Wix forum and Facebook posts for schedule changes, a whiteboard, an answering machine, punch cards, a cash box with envelope kiln tickets, an email list for firing dates (Belger).
6. **Orientation is recurring, scheduled and expiring** (12 sources; Canberra renews every 12 months and runs 3–4 sessions a month; UCSD valid one academic year; Sonoma requires a walkthrough even for returning members).
7. **Membership waitlists are long, manual queues** (7 sources: Kiln Rooms 2–5 months; Mississauga year-end openings and unpublished order; Merrill lottery; NCC; Waterloo full; Pottery NW re-queue on cancel; Mudflat interest list).
8. **Monitors and volunteers handle money and stock** (Arts Underground, Sonoma, Canberra events, Copper Country), so a shift is also a till session. This links the staffing, payments and inventory hypotheses.
9. **Wheel booking is not universal.** Turning Earth (3 London sites): "You don't need to book" and "equipment has always been sufficient"; GHP first-come with a sign-up "If needed"; Pottery NW and Recess have no reservations.
10. **Schedule-change notification is manual** at 7 studios (Fitton, Square Edge, Mudflat, Brookline, Belger, Mississauga, Turning Earth). This matches 03's finding that "ready" notifications are a pain point.
